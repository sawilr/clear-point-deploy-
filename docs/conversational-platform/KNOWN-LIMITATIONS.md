# Known Limitations (honest register)

**Date:** 2026-08-28. Not hidden, not blockers for MVP; each has a path.

1. **Rate limiting is per-serverless-instance** (RL-08). Burst across instances sees no 429. Mitigation in place: validation rejects junk anyway (live: 50-burst → 50×400, 0 accepted). Fix: Vercel KV/Upstash (owner provisions, code ready).
2. **No persistent metrics store / owner dashboard** (§110). [AI-AUDIT]/[SCOPE-AUDIT]/[LEAD-AUDIT] emit complete PII-free events, but Vercel function logs are ephemeral without a drain. Cost/KPI dashboards (§47–48) need KV or a log drain first. Emission layer is done; storage is the gap.
3. **The confidence-scored intent classifier (§4/§5) runs in SHADOW only** — it logs intent + confidence per turn ([AI-AUDIT]) but routes nothing; the narrower regex scope router remains the only actor. This is the spec's own §114 rule: observe false-positive rates in the field first, then decide per-intent what may act. Promotion to acting classifier = next phase, after log review.
4. **ALLOWED_STATES not centrally configurable** (§19): NY/NJ/CT lives in content + engine strings + prompt. Changing footprint today = coordinated edit + full regression; an admin-config refactor is next-phase.
5. **Voice policy staged, not applied** — live phone lines; needs the 15-minute owner window (backup → paste → 4 test calls). Until then wrong-number calls behave as before.
6. **Lead score (§17–18)** not emitted as a 0–100 CRM field; qualification is flow-based (Zara) + tags. Adding the score touches CRM schema — next-phase with owner's field mapping.
7. **Zara's engine is client-side** — its deterministic replies cost nothing but its logic is inspectable in the bundle (accepted trade-off; no secrets client-side; abuse lands on rate-limited APIs).
8. **Voice observability** limited to GHL's own logs/recordings; per-call closure-reason tagging depends on GHL workflow capabilities (see BLOCKED V2).
9. **4 engine F2 greeting checks** were failing pre-2026-08-28; fixed (345/345) — noted here because the fix merged two historically conflicting guards (see CHANGELOG).
10. **Pre-existing engine false positives** documented 2026-08-18 ("$911", "911 Broadway", "cover the emergency room?") — LOW, deliberately untouched (narrowing a life-safety net is the dangerous direction); dedicated pass scheduled.
11. **TPMO (e)(41) counts still missing** (master audit 2026-09-12, P1, owner-gated): the standardized multi-carrier sentence requires organization/product counts; the site renders the standardized sentences without them until the FMO supplies the figures (`src/lib/tpmoConfig.ts`).
12. **Voice greetings lack AI/recording disclosure** (owner window required — BLOCKED V3); post-call tagging workflow is a draft (V4).
13. **Node 20.18 locally vs engines >=20.19**: `npm install` prints EBADENGINE warnings on this workstation (Vercel builds on Node 24). Upgrade the local Node when convenient.
14. **Storage-denied browsers** now render fully (safeStorage) but keep no language/consent/chat memory across reloads — by design.
15. **The numeric backstop fails OPEN on comparisons** (red-team round 3, accepted): when a sentence or block names the current year AND another one, no figure in it is rewritten — a markdown table with 2026 and 2027 columns, `Part B deductible: $257 (2027: $300)`, or any sentence carrying "varies". A wrong current-year figure in that shape survives the backstop; the LLM prompt and the deterministic engine remain the primary controls. Chosen deliberately: a false correction (rewriting a correct next-year figure to this year's value during AEP) is worse than a miss.
16. **Amounts the backstop cannot see**: values with no `$`, no `dólares`/`dollars` and no `USD` prefix; figures stated as words ("two hundred eighty-three dollars"); and per-day/coinsurance amounts, which are deliberately never classified.
17. **Clara's explicit consent turn is latent** (TCPA-03, red-team round 3): the advisor-callback paths now require a discrete "Yes, I agree" before any submission, but the outer flow hands off to the LLM engine right after the ZIP question, so `path_select` and the Path A/C capture steps are not reachable in the shipped build. The fix is code- and type-verified, not browser-verified; it protects those paths if they are ever re-enabled.
18. **The adversarial loop did not converge.** Four independent red-team rounds ran against the remediation (62 / 118 / 168 / 61 documented probes, 409 in total) and all four found defects — 30, 31, 60 and 43 reports, 12 of them P1. The directive's "three consecutive clean passes" was not reached, and round 4's fixes have not themselves been attacked. Root cause is structural: these controls interpret natural language with regular expressions, so every narrowing or broadening opens a new edge. What the loop did buy is a permanent corpus — 384 checks across the three round-specific suites, one per reproduced defect.
19. **49 of the 109 first-pass findings are still open** (35 P3, 10 P4, 4 P2), classified by an independent agent and re-checked by a skeptic. They are code debt (the business phone hardcoded in 37 files, files over the project's own size rule), SEO detail and assistant polish. Remediation prioritised P0/P1/P2 and did not reach the long tail.
20. **`npm test` is offline-only**: `test-optout-validation` POSTs to the deployed `/api/opt-out` and trips its per-IP rate limit when repeated, so it runs only under `CP_LIVE=1` after a deploy.

## Round 5 (2026-09-14) — what the fifth adversarial round changed here

21. **Round 4's fixes made two controls worse, and only an independent round found it.** The fifth red team ran 52 documented attacks against the round-4 remediation and filed 59 defects: 2 P0, 15 P1, 20 P2, 15 P3, 7 P4. Five of the six P1s in the reply filter and all five in the figure backstop were **regressions** — behaviour that was correct at 419c5cd and was broken by a round-4 fix. The pattern was identical every time: a narrow pattern written against one adversarial string, never measured against the traffic it had to keep working for. Two were P0. The emergency post-condition could be satisfied by any 911-shaped token near an ordinary service verb, so a caller writing "I am having chest pain and I cannot breathe" could be answered with "Please call your plan at 9-1-1-ext 4 for that billing question." And the date-of-birth redaction sat behind a test on a field that four of the five lead surfaces never send, so it was dead code on most production traffic. Both are fixed, both now carry a suite built from the attack strings in both directions, and the lesson is recorded in the guardrail rule: **a control is not verified by its own author's suite.**

22. **Two guards are now deliberately default-deny, and will sometimes be "wrong" in the safe direction.** `carriesEmergencyInstruction` answers "yes" only when the number is the direct object of an instruction verb or carries an urgency marker of its own. An unusual phrasing of a correct emergency reply therefore loses its wording to the canonical one. That is the intended trade: a false accept can leave a billing question in front of a heart attack; a false reject substitutes a different correct emergency instruction. The consent classifier is the mirror image — refusal is read broadly and checked first, because a false decline costs a lead and a false agree is a TCPA violation.

23. **One filter case is knowingly left as a false positive.** "Do you want to know what it takes before you qualify for Extra Help?" is replaced by the compliant non-answer. The red team filed it alongside two cases that are now fixed, but its own recommended test — a speech or knowledge verb between the question opener and the claim — keeps this one removed too, because "know" is exactly that verb. Loosening the rule far enough to keep it would also admit "Do you know that you qualify for Extra Help?", which is a determination wearing a question mark. Pinned in `scripts/test-compliance-filter-r5-2026-09-14.mjs` so it stays a decision rather than drift.

24. **Clara's transcript is permanently shorter on a phone, and at 568px tall the entry screen still does not fit.** Making the recording and commission disclosure a persistent strip costs about 112px (English) to 129px (Spanish) of fixed height on `/support`, which is one of only two routes with no footer and therefore the sole carrier of that disclosure. Two pieces of chrome were reclaimed to pay for it — the composer during the language step, where it is inert by definition, and the call-status row on phones, which duplicates the site top bar's phone link. Measured result: greeting and language chips inside the visible transcript in 12 of 12 route and width combinations at 667px tall. At 568px tall (iPhone SE class) the chips still fall below the fold between 320px and 390px wide; closing that needs either the 139px site header or the mandatory TPMO paragraph, both outside this component. **Not verified whether that 568px case is a regression or pre-existing** — the pre-fix build was already gone when it was measured, and the arithmetic that says pre-existing is an inference, not evidence.

25. **The guards in the figure backstop are grammatical heuristics, not parsing.** A clause "has a competing subject" if a word matching a small verb list follows a noun phrase; a label is a "short lead-in" if it is four words or fewer; a caption is recognised by how it starts. Each rule was chosen because it separates the specific cases the red team produced, and each will mis-fire on constructions nobody has written yet. The correction band is now 20x rather than 3x and `$0` is always corrected, which is the right direction for the errors this module exists to catch, but it also means a genuinely unrelated amount inside the same clause as a Part keyword is more likely to be rewritten than it was.

26. **Three new build gates now stand between a defect and a deploy**, each written because the same class of failure had already happened twice: `check-allowlist-drift.mjs` (a server allow-list silently discarding what the front end sends — it found a third live instance on its first run), `check-zero-cost-claims.mjs` (a "$0 premium" claim drifting away from its cost qualifier), and the hardened `check-figures-year.mjs` (a decimal tail or a stale comment slipping a wrong figure past the year check). All three are proven against copies of the tree that contain the defect: each exits 1 and names it.
